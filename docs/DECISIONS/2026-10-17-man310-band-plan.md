# MAN-310: "all bands" skims the standard CW segments a receiver can tune

Date: 2026-10-17. Ticket: MAN-310. Builds on MAN-13
([2026-10-15-man13-multi-input-daemon.md](2026-10-15-man13-multi-input-daemon.md)).

## Problem

An operator gave each receiver one centre (`freq_hz`, in Hz). manta had no
list of where CW lives on each band, so covering a band wider than one
stream meant hand-writing several `[[input]]` entries. A station in the
overlap was then tracked and decoded by both streams, and MAN-13's D6
dedupe kept whichever report arrived first.

Reproduced at `e539c9b`: `bands = ["all"]` was `unknown field`, and
`freq_hz = "7030"` a type error. Two 96 kS/s inputs at 21 037.5 and
21 112.5 kHz (the 15 m split below) with DL1ABC at 21 070 kHz and JA1XYZ at
21 080 kHz rendered into both promoted four tracks: input 1 at
21 070 029.6 Hz and input 0 at 21 079 970.8 Hz as well as the nearer ones.
D6 spotted each station once, but the nearer stream won only by arrival
order.

## Decisions

- **D1 One table.** `manta_server::band_plan::CW_SEGMENTS` holds the ten
  rows (band, CW low and high, optional NCDXF beacon). docs/CONFIG.md's
  band table mirrors it, and `config_md_band_table_matches_cw_segments`
  holds the two equal; `band_plan_beacons_are_the_calibrate_beacons` holds
  the beacons equal to `manta_engine::calibrate::REFERENCES`.
- **D2 Skimmed span** = [CW low, max(CW high, beacon)]. 15 m's span
  (21 000 to 21 150 kHz) is the only 150 kHz row, matching the ticket's
  split scenario.
- **D3 Vocabulary.** `bands` is a string, a number or a list of `"all"`,
  band names (case-insensitive) and bare centres in kHz. `freq_hz` keeps Hz,
  so nothing existing changes meaning. A bare centre inside a listed band,
  a band listed twice and a band `"all"` already includes are refused.
- **D4 Width and split.** A stream's width is one KiwiSDR channel
  (`manta_input::kiwi::IQ_PASSBAND_HZ`, 10 kHz) or `rate_hz`, capped by
  `capture_rate_hz`. It skims 2 × `TRUSTED_HALF_BAND_FRACTION` (0.4526) ×
  the width, MAN-196's <= 1 dB band. A span gets ⌈span / usable⌉ equal
  slices, centred on whole Hz. "all" on 0 to 30 MHz is 14 streams at
  96 kS/s, 11 at 192 kS/s, 20 at 48 kS/s and 86 on Kiwi channels.
- **D5 Tuning range.** `tuning_range_hz`, default `[0, 30 MHz]`, only with
  `bands`. A band outside it is skipped, one partly outside is clipped, a
  bare centre outside is skipped, each with a note; nothing left is an
  error.
- **D6 Ownership.** Among one entry's streams, sorted by centre, two whose
  usable bands overlap split the overlap at the midpoint of their centres,
  plus one channel (93.75 Hz, `SPLIT_GUARD_HZ`, held equal to
  `CHANNEL_SPACING_HZ`). The share is intersected with the trusted band in
  `run_input_front` (`spawn_veto_band`) and vetoes spawns only, so a
  running track is never closed (MAN-196/MAN-314). A double decode within
  the guard strip is inside D6's one-channel cross-input tolerance, so it
  is one spot. `a_split_bands_overlap_signal_is_decoded_once_by_the_nearer_stream`
  and `a_signal_on_the_split_point_is_spotted_once` pin it.
- **D7 Expansion in the loader.** An entry with `bands` becomes one input
  per stream: a copy with `freq_hz` = the centre, checked by
  `validate_input` like a hand-written entry. A one-stream plan is
  identical to `freq_hz = <centre>` and takes the single-input path.
- **D8 Capacity.** A Kiwi entry opens any number of streams; an HPSDR or
  SoapySDR entry opens one, and a larger plan is refused naming it; all
  inputs stay within manta's 16. ka9q, file and audio reject `bands`, so
  file-input determinism is untouched.
- **D9 Names.** A one-stream plan keeps the entry name; several streams are
  `<entry>-<label>` (`kiwi-60m-1`, `kiwi-7030`), within 32 characters and
  unique among all inputs.
- **D10 Words.** The plan and every skip or clip are loader notes, printed
  before every command's output. Each split stream logs one INFO line
  after open: `input kiwi-60m-1: new tracks start only from 5350.25 to
  5359.094 kHz; …`, in exact kHz (`band_plan::exact_khz`).
- **D11 calibrate saves into the entry.** `NamedInput.entry` names the
  entry a stream came from; `calibrate --input kiwi-60m-1` saves into it
  and the prompt says `(all N streams of this receiver)`.
- **D12 Unchanged.** `input.bands` is restart-only through the `input.*`
  rule (`changing_input_bands_needs_a_restart`). Spots, telnet, JSON, the
  RBN uplink and SETT are unchanged.
- **D13 Kiwi connects per channel.** `kiwi.rs` allowed one outstanding
  connect per `host:port`, so the second stream of a plan failed to open
  (`TCP connect to … skipped: a previous attempt's DNS resolution or
  connect is still running`, seen in `bands_open_one_kiwi_session_per_planned_stream`).
  The key now includes the tuned frequency: each stream's own retries stay
  bounded and sibling streams open side by side.

## Reviewer questions, defaults built

1. A receiver that opens fewer streams than the plan needs (one HPSDR
   radio, one SoapySDR device) is refused with the plan named, not
   truncated: silently skimming fewer bands than "all" promises would
   contradict the setting. "all" on one HL2 runs unchanged once MAN-307
   lands.
2. The segment numbers are the table above: 80 m 3500 to 3580 (IARU R1 CW
   plus the R2 skimming practice), 160 m 1810 to 1840, 60 m 5351.5 to
   5366.5 (WRC-15), spans widened to the beacon. A change is one row plus
   its test expectations.
3. Ownership applies only among one entry's own streams; overlapping
   hand-written entries keep D6's first-report-wins (diversity is
   MAN-14/MAN-16's).

## Not yet

- Several receivers on one HPSDR radio (MAN-307), several streams from one
  SoapySDR device, and radiod channels created from `bands` (the MAN-140
  follow-up).
- Operator-defined segments, a per-region table, 6 m and up, 2200/630 m.
- A `--bands` flag; probing a receiver's real tuning range.
- A warning for `freq_hz = 7030` (Hz, almost certainly meant as kHz).
- Nearer-centre ownership across separate entries.
